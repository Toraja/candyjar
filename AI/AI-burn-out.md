> "You don’t work less. You just work the same amount or even more."
---

That line comes from an engineer interviewed in the UC Berkeley study published in Harvard Business Review(Feb 2026). It captures a key theme of the research: even though AI tools make certain tasks faster, people rarely use the saved time to rest or reduce effort.

Instead, the perceived efficiency gain often drives people to do more—taking on new projects, expanding responsibilities, or refining existing work—so total working hours and mental load stay the same or increase. The researchers call this paradox “workload creep,” where productivity boosts don’t translate into rest but into higher expectations and added tasks.
